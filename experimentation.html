Helping forecasting scientists launch, track, and review machine-learning experiments independently.

PRODUCT MISSION

Help scientists spend more of their time learning from forecasting experiments.

Problem & goal

Before scientists could train, fine-tune, or evaluate a model, they had to configure and orchestrate the experiment. Repeating this setup added work before the modeling could begin.

The goal: make those repeatable steps easier to launch, track, and reuse, with a clear path to the outputs.

Solution

A self-service workflow that lets scientists request training, fine-tuning, inference, and backtesting through APIs and natural language. The platform orchestrates the jobs and exposes run status and results.

User journey

My role

I designed and drove delivery of the platform workflows and agent tools, connecting configuration, orchestration, status checks, and output retrieval. I also mentored an intern contributing to the implementation.

Decisions

Make the full task self-service.

A launch button alone would leave scientists waiting without knowing what happened. I included status checks and result retrieval so they could follow an experiment through to its output.

Connect the agent to repeatable workflows.

The natural-language interface sat on top of APIs and orchestrated jobs. This connected a simpler entry point to the execution steps needed for training, inference, and evaluation.

Separate setup effort from execution time.

The platform could remove repeated configuration work, while model compute time still depended on the experiment. Keeping those separate made the improvement more useful to assess.

Impact

~90%Less experiment setup effort. The improvement covers preparation and launch, rather than model compute time.
20–25Experiment jobs per day using the workflow. This is a usage measure, rather than a count of unique scientists.

Reported delivery outcomes. A measurement window and instrumentation breakdown are not included in this summary.

How I would track continued success

Compare setup time for similar experiment types, track weekly active scientists and repeat use, and measure the share of runs that reach accessible results. Use run failures and requests for engineering help as guardrails.

Proposed follow-up measures, separate from the reported results above.

What I took away

For an internal platform, the product is the whole task. Reliable status and accessible results are what let people use it independently.
