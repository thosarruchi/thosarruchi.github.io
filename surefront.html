Bringing early ideation into Surefront.

A new ideation workflow for creative teams, before a product has a complete specification.

My roleProduct Manager
Team4-person CMU industry studio
WhenJanuary–December 2024
Problem & goalSolutionUser journeyMy roleDecisionsImpact

PRODUCT MISSION

Help retailers and brands develop a product from its first spark of inspiration.

Problem & goal

Surefront’s existing workflow supported products once their specifications were taking shape. But a creative’s first idea might just be a photo, a PDF, or a slide deck.

Our goal: let teams capture inspiration and develop it into a structured product concept within Surefront.

Solution

A Trend Board where creative teams can capture inspiration, organize assets, and review ideas together. Product details can be added as the concept becomes clearer, so teams do not need a finished specification to get started.

User journey

My role

I worked with client stakeholders to understand the gap between early creative work and Surefront’s existing product workflow. Through client meetings, Figma walkthroughs, and reviews of the application, I identified missing steps and translated them into product requirements and a prioritized roadmap.

My role was to make the choices clear: what a creative team needed to do first, which requests mattered most, and what could wait. I used demos and client testing to refine the workflow and align our four-person studio team with the client’s goals.

Decisions

Let people start with an incomplete idea.

Requiring product details too early would make the first step harder for creatives. We separated capturing inspiration from completing specifications, so a photo, PDF, or slide deck could be a starting point.

Trade-off: accept less structure at the start and let teams add detail as the concept develops.

Use feedback to clarify the workflow.

The designs did not answer every question about how the feature would fit into the existing application. I used client walkthroughs, demos, and testing feedback to identify missing steps and dependencies and refine the requirements.

Research scope: discovery centered on client stakeholders, existing designs, and application behavior.

Prioritize a focused first version.

The backlog was larger than the team could take on. I used MoSCoW prioritization and dependencies to narrow the scope and make the priorities explicit.

Trade-off: a smaller initial feature set, with a clearer commitment around the new ideation workflow.

Impact

The Trend Board introduced an earlier starting point within Surefront. Creative teams could bring in inspiration, organize and review it together, and develop a concept before completing product specifications.

Client feedback and testing shaped the resulting workflow. The product outcome was a new place for early ideation within the existing product lifecycle.

How I would measure product success

The next question is whether teams reach a useful concept faster and continue using the workflow. These are proposed follow-up KPIs, not reported post-launch results.

Activation
Share of new teams that create a board, add assets, and get a second teammate contributing within 7 days.
Time to concept
Median time from the first asset to a concept ready for specification.
Retention
Share of activated teams contributing to a board again in week 4.
Guardrails
Upload success rate and time until an asset has a usable preview.

Measurement windows should follow the creative teams’ actual planning cadence.

What I took away

I learned to connect each requirement to the creative team’s next step. Next time, I would observe creatives directly earlier and define activation and time-to-concept measures alongside the roadmap.
